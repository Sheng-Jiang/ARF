# ARF review — 13 Sep 2026

A read of the whole system against the research disciplines we set out for the

## 1. What is genuinely good

This is well above hobby grade and the review should start by saying so.

- **204 unit tests**, ruff-clean, typed throughout, containerised, scheduled on
  Cloud Run. The test suite runs in under four seconds.
- **The two-factor design is sound.** Exposure × valuation combined as a
  geometric mean means a name has to score on both to reach D1. That is the
  right shape — it rules out "expensive but not an AI story" and "pure AI story
  but cheap" in one step.
- **Legs are scored separately and never pooled.** Correct, and resisted in
  most retail tooling.
- **The FX fix is subtle and right.** Spotting that ADRs trade in USD but
  report in CNY/TWD, and that keying off the trading rate inflates FCF yield
  ~7x (~32x for TSM) for the largest China-leg names, is the kind of error that
  survives for years in professional models. `_local_market_cap` is the best
  code in the repo.
- **`CALIBRATION_TARGETS` is the instinct that matters most.** Encoding "NVDA
  must land D2–D5", "GOOGL must not sit alone at D1" as a testable gate is
  exactly the discipline of writing down what you expect before you look. Very
  few people build this. It currently runs **10/12 green** (below).
- Domain care is visible: 扣非 net profit for Chinese ROE, the Cambricon +2386%
  growth cap, `citations_json` on the Gemini summaries.

## 2. The structural problem: ARF cannot measure a bubble

`ARF = sqrt(E × V)` where E and V are each percentile ranks within leg, and
the result is percentile-ranked again. A percentile rank has the same
distribution every time it is computed. From the database:

| as_of | leg | n | median ARF | mean ARF | min | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-05-28 | US | 16 | 53.1 | 53.1 | 6.3 | 100.0 |
| 2026-06-26 | US | 36 | 50.7 | 51.4 | 2.8 | 100.0 |
| 2026-06-30 | US | 36 | 50.7 | 51.4 | 2.8 | 100.0 |
| 2026-07-19 | US | 36 | 51.4 | 51.4 | 2.8 | 100.0 |
| 2026-05-28 | China | 13 | 53.9 | 53.9 | 7.7 | 96.2 |
| 2026-07-19 | China | 37 | 50.0 | 51.4 | 2.7 | 100.0 |

Median ~51 in every snapshot, every leg, mechanically. The whole AI complex
could double or halve and this table would not change. **A thermometer that
reads 50° every week is not measuring temperature.**

ARF answers a real and useful question — *which name is most stretched relative
to its peers today* — but that is a ranking, not a level, and the product is
named and framed as a level. The dashboard's absolute lines (froth count,
median EV/S, implied growth gap) were bolted on to compensate; the third of
those is null in every row (see §4).

**This is the same failure the curriculum is built to prevent**: a relative
ordering never tells you what the price assumes, only who assumes more.

## 3. The reverse DCF is not one

```python
def reverse_dcf(price, fcf, wacc):
    return wacc - fcf / price
```

Two consequences, both material:

1. **It is a monotone transform of FCF yield.** `g* = wacc − FCF/P`, and
   V_score component C1 percentile-ranks it. Ranking is invariant to monotone
   transforms, so **C1 is exactly an inverted FCF-yield rank**. The most
   sophisticated-sounding component in the model carries no information beyond
   `FCF/P`. The single-stage form also bounds `g*` below WACC by construction,
   compressing every fast grower into a narrow band.
2. **`implied_growth_gap = g* − eps_2yr_cagr` subtracts unlike things** — a
   perpetual FCF growth rate minus a 1–2 year EPS growth rate. For any fast
   grower it is a large negative number driven mostly by the consensus term.

### What replaced it

`arf/implied.py` (new, 22 tests in `tests/test_implied.py`, all passing,
ruff-clean, nothing else touched) solves the multi-stage form by bisection:

```
EV = Σ(t=1..N) FCF₀(1+g)^t/(1+w)^t  +  FCF₀(1+g)^N(1+g_t) / [(w−g_t)(1+w)^N]
```

for `g` — the FCF CAGR the current price requires over an explicit 10-year
window before fading to terminal. The output is **absolute and in real units**,
so a median across the leg moves when the market moves. Run over the existing
database with no other change:

| as_of | leg | coverage | median implied 10y | p75 | share > 25% | median ARF |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-05-28 | US | 0.75 | **26.4%** | 31.7% | 0.58 | 53.1 |
| 2026-06-26 | US | 0.81 | **22.9%** | 31.2% | 0.45 | 50.7 |
| 2026-06-30 | US | 0.81 | **23.1%** | 32.0% | 0.45 | 50.7 |
| 2026-07-19 | US | 0.78 | **23.0%** | 30.2% | 0.46 | 51.4 |
| 2026-05-28 | China | 0.25 | **15.6%** | 24.0% | 0.25 | 53.8 |
| 2026-07-19 | China | 0.11 | **23.8%** | 34.2% | 0.50 | 50.0 |

The US leg cooled from late May and flattened; the China leg heated from 15.6%
to 23.8%. That is a signal ARF structurally could not produce, from data you
already had. (The China figures rest on 4 names — see §5. `froth_level` returns
`coverage` alongside every statistic for exactly this reason.)

Per name, latest snapshot:

| ticker | ARF | D | fwd P/E | ROE | **implied 10y FCF CAGR** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LITE | 86.1 | 2 | 40.0 | 0.23 | **56%** |
| TSLA | 97.2 | 1 | 149.1 | 0.05 | **44%** |
| PLTR | 88.9 | 2 | 63.2 | 0.33 | **38%** |
| GOOGL | 100.0 | 1 | 23.7 | 0.39 | **35%** |
| NVDA | 69.4 | 4 | 15.8 | 1.14 | **30%** |
| AMD | 80.6 | 2 | 36.8 | 0.08 | **31%** |

Three things fall out immediately that the ranking hid:

- **Lumentum's price requires 56% annual FCF growth for a decade** — the most
  extreme requirement in the universe, sitting at D2. This is the 1999–2001
  optical case, live, in your own data.
- **NVDA and AMD both require ~30%**, at ROE of 114% and 8% respectively. Same
  demand, utterly different capacity to meet it. That contrast is the entire
  "what's in the price" lesson in one row.
- **GOOGL ranks #1 on ARF but 5th on what its price actually requires.**

## 4. Quality mega-caps score too frothy — and the system already knows

`calibration_check` on the latest snapshot: **10 of 12 pass**.

```
NVDA      expect D2-D5   actual D4   OK        BABA      expect D6-D10  actual D8   OK
PLTR      expect D1-D2   actual D2   OK        0700.HK   expect D6-D10  actual D10  OK
LITE      expect D1-D3   actual D2   OK        300394.SZ expect D1-D3   actual D2   OK
CSCO      expect D6-D10  actual D8   OK        688256.SH expect D1-D3   actual D2   OK
NEE       expect D6-D10  actual D10  OK        PANW      expect D1-D3   actual D1   OK
BIDU      expect D5-D10  actual D3   FAIL      GOOGL     expect D2-D8   actual D1   FAIL
```

Both failures are the same shape: large, profitable, reasonably-priced
platforms scoring as froth. GOOGL at forward P/E 23.7, P/S 10, ROE 39% is the
single frothiest name in the US leg. Likely causes, in order:

1. **`ev_sales_5yr_percentile` compares a company to its own history.** A
   quality compounder at its own multiple high is flagged as stretched
   regardless of absolute cheapness. This component is doing the most damage.
2. **The ROE quality adjustment is capped at ±20 points and then washed out**
   by the final percentile rank, which re-uniformises everything.
3. **Quality enters twice with opposite signs**: `gross_margin` is 20% of
   E_score (as a "supply-chain criticality" proxy) while ROE is subtracted in
   V_score.

The gate prints to console and does not block. Two known failures have been
sitting green-adjacent for weeks.

## 5. Four defects that undermine anything built on top

**5.1 History is mutable — the look-ahead trap.**
`upsert_snapshot` runs `DELETE FROM snapshots WHERE as_of_date = ?` then
`INSERT`. Re-running any past date silently replaces that week's record with
today's data view, including restated fundamentals. There is no `fetched_at`,
no vintage, no revision. This is precisely the defect that makes a backtest
worthless — and `webapp/backtest.py` exists. **Nothing built on this history
can be trusted until the table is append-only.**

**5.2 The universe moves under the scores.**
Snapshot counts run 13 → 16 → 12 → 37 → 31 → 37. Percentile ranks are computed
within whichever names resolved that week, so a name's ARF moves when its peers
fail to fetch. Cross-time comparison of any percentile is confounded.

**5.3 Coverage is low and silent.**
Latest snapshot, 86 rows: 13 have no price or market cap at all; `free_cash_flow`
is null for 39 (45%); `net_income_excl_nr` null for 61; `revenue_ttm` null for 38.
The failures are absorbed into the ranking without comment.

The sharpest consequence: **every Chinese optical name is unresolvable.**
300308 (InnoLight), 300502 (Eoptolink), 300394 (TFC), 688498 (源杰) all lack
usable FCF, so the tool cannot price the companies at the centre of your own
thesis. China leg coverage is 0.11. Fixing the A-share FCF fetch is the single
highest-value data task in the repo.

**5.4 No forecast record.**
The system emits a score and a report weekly and keeps nothing that can be
graded later. `gemini_summaries` and `research_synthesis` store prose; neither
is scoreable. So the system cannot improve, and neither can its operator.

## 6. What to do, in order

**P0 — Make history immutable.** Add `fetched_at` and `data_vintage` to
`snapshots`; make writes append-only with a `revision` column and expose a
`latest_revision` view. One schema change; unblocks backtest validity,
calibration-over-time, and forecast scoring. Nothing else on this list is
worth doing first.

**P1 — Wire in `arf/implied.py`.** Add `implied_growth_multistage` to the
snapshot schema, populate it in `compute_arf`, and make the thermometer plot
`froth_level()` median and `share_above` instead of a flat percentile. Keep ARF
as the cross-sectional rank it genuinely is — and rename the chart so the two
questions stop being conflated: *who is most stretched* vs *how stretched is
the complex*.

**P2 — Fix the A-share FCF fetch.** Coverage 0.11 on the China leg makes half
the product decorative. This is plumbing, not modelling, and it is the task
that makes the tool answer your actual question.

**P3 — Fix the quality bias, then make the gate hard.** Drop or reweight
`ev_sales_5yr_percentile` in favour of an absolute anchor; apply the ROE
adjustment after the final rank; consider a floor rule (a name under ~25x
forward earnings with ROE > cost of equity cannot be D1). Then make
`calibration_check` fail CI rather than print.

**P4 — A `forecasts` table.** Each run, for every D1 name, write the implied
growth, a falsifiable statement, and the date it resolves. Add a grading job.
This is the decision journal and the monthly calibration exercise from the
learning track, automated and attached to real data — and it is the only part
of this system that compounds.

**P5 — Circularity.** `config/value_chain.yaml` and the 双价值链 page already
have the scaffolding. Add a `funding_edges` config — who has taken equity in,
committed capex to, or prepaid whom — and compute per name the share of revenue
attributable to counterparties inside the loop. That number is the real
cyclicality of a position and appears in no sell-side model. You are two files
away from an analysis nobody else is running.

**P6 — Extend provenance beyond the LLM layer.** `citations_json` has the right
instinct; fundamentals have only a `data_source` string. Carry a per-field
source so the report renderer can enforce "no number without a link".

**P7 — Re-run the pipeline.** The local DB's last snapshot is 2026-07-19, while
`implied_growth` and `cohort` landed 2026-08-09 — so both are null in every
local row and the newest features have never executed here. Worth confirming
whether the deployed Cloud Run job is current, since the local copy is a dev
artefact.

## 8. Notes for whoever implements this

**Do P0 before P1.** Wiring the new metric into a table whose history is
overwritten on every re-run bakes the mutability problem in deeper — you will
have a level series you cannot trust and will not be able to reconstruct. The
schema change is small and it is genuinely first.

**Do not fix §4 by widening `CALIBRATION_TARGETS`.** The two failures are
evidence that the model has a quality bias. Loosening the bands until they pass
converts a working instrument into a rubber stamp, and it is the single most
tempting wrong move in this whole list. If a target is genuinely wrong, change
it in a separate commit with the reasoning written down — never in the same
commit as a scoring change.

**`arf/implied.py` is a first draft, not a finished answer.** Three judgment
parameters are hardcoded and should move to config: the 10-year window, the
terminal growth rates (3% US / 4% China), and the 25% froth threshold in
`froth_level`. None of them is measured; all three change the output. The
solver bounds and the None-on-out-of-range behaviour are deliberate — resist
any change that returns a number where the model has stopped being meaningful.

**Keep ARF.** The recommendation is not to delete it. It answers a real
question well; it is just not the question the chart claims. Two metrics,
clearly labelled, is the fix — not one metric doing both jobs badly.

**Test environment.** The committed `.venv` is macOS-built. The non-network
suite needs `pandas numpy duckdb pyyaml pytest`; the rest of the suite also
wants `yfinance streamlit plotly rich tenacity baostock`, whose absence shows
up as eight collection errors that are environmental, not real failures.

**Verification bar for P1.** After wiring, the median implied growth series
must differ across snapshot dates. If it comes out flat, the metric has been
percentile-ranked somewhere by accident and the whole exercise has been undone
— that is the one regression test that matters.
